# Image and video craft vocabulary

Use this vocabulary in everyday prompt preparation, selected for the intended image or shot. Pair a term with a concrete visible effect: “hard rim light tracing the hair against a black background” is more actionable than “cinematic lighting.” Rich detail is welcome when coherent. Do not insert every term into every prompt, force an editorial look onto a character master, or import example subjects into another project.

## Framing and composition — images and video

| Term | Meaning and useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Establishing / wide shot | Show the setting and subject's position within it; specify scale and placement. |
| Medium shot / bust portrait | Frame a person roughly waist-up / head and upper torso; state the intended crop. |
| Close-up / extreme close-up | Emphasize a face, object or small detail; name what fills the frame. |
| Profile / three-quarter view | Side view / angled view showing front and side; respect workflow-owned camera controls. |
| Low / high angle | Look upward / downward at the subject; describe the desired sense of scale. |
| POV / third-person | View through a character's eyes / observe the character externally. A camera chasing behind a rider is third-person, not their POV. |
| Silhouette / negative space | Readable dark outline against light / deliberately unoccupied space around the subject. |
| Foreground, midground, background | Separate depth layers and relationships; specify which subject overlaps which. |

## Camera and timing — video

| Term | Meaning and useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Oner / continuous take | One uninterrupted shot, no cuts. Describe a connected camera path from start to finish. |
| Tracking / chase camera | Camera travels with / pursues the subject. Specify distance, height and relative position. |
| Dolly / push-in / pull-back | Camera physically moves through space, closer to / farther from the subject. A zoom changes framing through focal length; these are different effects. |
| Orbit | Camera travels around a subject; specify side, direction and endpoint. Can remain one continuous take. |
| Pan / tilt / roll | Rotate the view horizontally / vertically / around the viewing axis. Distinguish camera roll from a creature rolling. |
| Whip pan | Rapid horizontal camera rotation with a brief blurred sweep; do not accidentally describe a cut when continuity matters. |
| Steadicam-style fluidity / handheld | Smooth stabilized traveling motion / intentional human sway or shake. Specify the amount. |
| Slow motion | Action unfolds slower than normal playback; specify which action and interval. |
| Speed ramp | Change perceived action speed within a shot, such as full speed → extreme slow motion → full speed. Distinguish this from camera acceleration. |
| Near-standstill / freeze frame | Very slow continuing movement / held still image. Name which is intended. |
| Beat / endpoint | An observable action stage / final image or action that the shot reaches. Use ordered, non-overlapping timings within the selected duration. |

Still images can imply movement through pose, particles and blur, but cannot perform an actual camera move or speed ramp. For video, keep screen time distinct from story time: slow motion consumes part of the selected clip duration. If several actions and a dramatic reveal overcrowd five seconds, offer a simpler choreography or a longer duration for review; do not silently change settings or remove requested beats.

## Lens, focus and texture — images and video

| Term | Meaning and useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Wide-angle / telephoto look | Broad spatial view with strong near/far size differences / narrow view often paired with distant-camera compressed perspective. Specify framing and camera distance, not a lens adjective alone. |
| Shallow / deep depth of field | Narrow / broad zone of acceptable focus. Name the focal subject and desired background softness. |
| Rack focus | Shift focus between specified depth planes during a shot; not an actual time-based effect in a still. |
| Sharp subject / motion blur | Keep a named subject crisp while allowing moving wings, particles or background to streak. Avoid requesting every plane sharp alongside a very shallow focus plane. |
| Anamorphic flare | Stylized lens flare often described as horizontal light streaks; tie it to a visible bright source. |
| Film grain / skin texture | Fine photographic texture / natural surface detail. Describe strength; neither means adding random dirt or oversharpening. |
| 35mm film look | Photographic aesthetic involving grain, contrast and color response; distinct from a 35mm focal length. “Film quality,” “UHD” and “hyperdetailed” do not set actual output resolution or guarantee fidelity. |

## Light and color — images and video

| Term | Meaning and useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Key / fill light | Main shaping light / light that lifts shadows. Specify direction and relative strength. |
| Rim / backlight | Light outlining subject edges / light from behind the subject. Useful for separating pale hair or wing membranes from a dark field. |
| Hard / soft light | Crisp-edged / gradual shadows. Specify source direction and which surfaces it shapes. |
| Chiaroscuro / high contrast | Dramatic modeling through light and shadow / strong bright-dark separation. Preserve readable focal details. |
| Low-key / high-key | Predominantly dark composition with selective light / predominantly bright composition with restrained shadows. Not camera viewing angles. |
| Monochrome | One color family; specify black and white when intended. Do not accidentally add colored highlights to a strict monochrome request. |
| Warm–cool separation | Contrast warm and cool regions, such as pale-gold wing light against steel-blue storm clouds. Name where each belongs. |
| Color grading | Overall palette, tonal balance and contrast treatment. “Professional grading” alone gives little direction; describe the desired treatment. |

## Atmosphere, materials and effects — images and video

| Term | Meaning and useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Volumetric fog / light shafts | Fog with visible depth and light scattering / beams revealed by atmosphere. Keep the important silhouette readable. |
| Atmospheric perspective | Distant layers lose contrast and detail through haze, helping establish scale. |
| Bioluminescence / reflection | A subject emits light / returns light from another source. Specify glowing vein locations and whether they illuminate nearby surfaces. |
| Translucency | Light passes through a material while being scattered, such as thin butterfly or creature wing membranes; distinct from clear transparency. |
| Spindrift / suspended particles | Wind-driven snow or spray / particles hanging or moving slowly. Specify direction, size and relation to the camera. |
| Practical / fantastical effects | Physically grounded observable effects / invented phenomena. Describe their appearance and interaction, rather than relying on a VFX label alone. |

## Sound — video where the selected workflow supports it

| Term | Meaning and useful direction |
| --- | --- |
| Diegetic / non-diegetic | Sound belonging to the scene / added score or other sound outside its world. |
| Ambience / Foley | Environmental bed such as wind / specific physical-action sounds such as harness creaks or footsteps. |
| Dialogue foreground | Exact speech remains intelligible above ambience and score. Name the speaker and preserve approved words. |

## Applying the owner's examples

The butterfly portrait combines a close-up profile/bust composition, hard rim light, black-and-white contrast, translucent wings and restrained film grain. These describe different visual decisions; do not substitute a pile of “high quality” synonyms for them.

The owner's [frost-glider prompt](../../video-prompting/references/owner-frost-glider.md) combines a continuous third-person chase, a connected sideward camera move, warm–cool separation, a reveal, a speed ramp and a diving endpoint. Keep the no-cut continuity when translating it to the selected H3 adapter. Creature negatives distinguish its original silhouette; they are not a universal fantasy prompt template. Its dense five-second choreography is a requested example, not verified generation evidence or a guarantee of achievable timing.

Actual duration, aspect ratio, resolution and reference bindings come from the workflow controls. Flag conflicts between those settings and prompt prose for review. Apply model-specific syntax and local limits after choosing the craft direction. Explain unfamiliar terms briefly when useful; do not require the owner to learn this glossary to use the app.
