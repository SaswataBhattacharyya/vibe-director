## Queue-pressure follow-up — 09:29 UTC

Further review found that `video_repertoire.start_job` spawned one worker process for each Media Prep request; all those processes then waited on the workload lease. This serialized actual work but could still grow CPU/RAM pressure with a large queue. It now persists each job in `queued` state and submits only the job ID to a single dispatcher thread. The dispatcher launches one subprocess and waits for it to exit before taking the next durable queued job. Queued jobs remain cancellable before launch; dispatched jobs retain their owned PID. A cancellation during the small process-start/PID-save window remains pending until the dispatcher attaches and stops the worker. Workers check cancellation before and after acquiring the lease. When the dispatcher is first started after an API restart, it also scans and resumes durable queued records. Existing production image/video consumers were already single-worker loops.
