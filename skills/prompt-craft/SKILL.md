## Clarify before drafting
For an underspecified request such as "a man", ask one compact question covering the decisions that matter: intended use, subject/appearance, setting/action, visual style, framing and light. Offer two or three directions when useful. Do not ask again for facts already in the conversation, screenplay or project knowledge. If the owner delegates creative choices, offer a concrete editable draft and label those choices as suggestions rather than established canon.

Use discuss while awaiting these choices; do not prepare a tool proposal yet. A request to use the existing prompt unchanged must remain possible. Direct manual Generate never requires a Director conversation.

## Craft and feedback
Use the shared [image/video craft glossary](references/visual-glossary.md) regularly when preparing prompts. Choose relevant terms and describe their intended effect; do not wait for the owner to supply professional vocabulary. Preserve meaningful cinematic detail rather than replacing it with generic quality adjectives. For images use framing, light, materials and composition; for video also use camera paths, choreography, timing and sound. Respect the selected model adapter and workflow constraints.

Choose details for their visible or audible effect, rather than making every prompt longer. Separate subject, composition, light, palette/materials and mood. Remove contradictions and repeated quality adjectives. Preserve established asset-role constraints: a white-background character master differs from an editorial portrait or a scene frame. Local workflow limits override examples.

Use approved project knowledge as persistent memory, scoped by model/workflow and intended use. On feedback, distinguish what the owner liked from a hypothesis about why it worked. Suggest a reusable lesson with model, context, successful/failed direction, relevant settings/reference identities and evidence when available. Ask the owner to review and save it in Context & skills → Project knowledge; never claim this conversation alone saved a lesson. Prompt Preparation also offers its existing reviewed learning-rule workflow. Do not overwrite application/global skills from a chat or carry a project's preferences into another project.

A liked output is evidence for that example, not proof that a phrase caused the result. Compare controlled variants only when authorized; never launch generation to learn automatically. This is guidance reuse, not model-weight training. Do not claim to have inspected an image/audio/video unless the runtime supplied actual perception evidence.
